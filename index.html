My story

From a lonely boarding-school kid to a global youth movement

Childhood

A boarding school in Dhaka

My parents were always busy, so I grew up in a school hostel. I was bullied, often lonely, and had very little sense of my own worth. I did well enough in class, but I knew almost nothing about life outside those walls, or about how many young people it was leaving behind.

2014

Four days after a loss

That year my friend Shomy Hasan Chowdhury lost her mother to complications from severe diarrhoea, an illness that clean water and basic hygiene can prevent. Four days later, the two of us ran our first water, sanitation and hygiene project. That was the beginning of Awareness 360.

2017–2019

Starting over in Malaysia

After a few semesters of economics in Dhaka, I transferred to Universiti Putra Malaysia. I lacked confidence and practical skills, so I said yes to everything: the Hult Prize, TEDxUPM, the Commonwealth Students’ Association. My family didn’t see volunteering as a career, so I worked part-time jobs to fund our projects and kept my grades up.

In 2019 I graduated with Second Class Upper Honours and became the first international student to receive UPM’s Putra Icon Award.

Shut out

Registered abroad, and growing anyway

At home, our applications to register as an NGO were rejected again and again. We had no political ties, but an official pointed to my grandfather’s past as an opposition activist. We had no bank access and no government support, so we registered in Malaysia and kept working. Being pushed out of Bangladesh is what made us global.

2021–2022

Recognition, and a bigger table

In 2021 Shomy and I were named to the Forbes 30 Under 30 Asia list for Social Impact, and I received The Diana Award. I advised the UN Global Compact’s Target Gender Equality programme, working with 700+ companies in 45+ countries, and led programmes for the Commonwealth Youth Forum in Kigali.

Today

Building the next generation

Awareness 360 now runs fellowships for young people from more than 100 countries, including Afghanistan, Palestine and Yemen, and has supported over 2,000 youth-led projects. At Acid Survivors Foundation I raised USD 300,000 in seven months for survivors of acid and gender-based violence. I’m now studying for an MA in Human Rights at the University of London on a Commonwealth scholarship.

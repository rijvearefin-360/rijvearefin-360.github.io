The story

From Mohammadpur to Mukono, and back again

Tap to see the canal near home before and after our Khale Hobe clean-up.

I

Where it started

Mohammadpur, Dhaka

I grew up in Mohammadpur, where litter, posters and banners covered everything and the air made me sick. Most of my childhood was spent in a boarding-school hostel. My parents were busy, I was bullied, and I had very little sense of my own worth.

I watched quietly and felt overwhelmed. In the end I left Bangladesh to study economics in Malaysia, looking for a better life.

“Hygiene is not a choice, it’s a habit.” An Awareness 360 hygiene campaign.

II

Four days after a loss

2014

My friend Shomy Hasan Chowdhury lost her mother to complications from severe diarrhoea, an illness that clean water and basic hygiene can prevent. Four days later, the two of us ran our first water, sanitation and hygiene project.

We called it Awareness 360. It started with two students and a hygiene workshop, and grew into a movement.

A guest lecture at BRAC University, and an Awareness 360 workshop with young changemakers.

III

Shut out, so we went global

Kuala Lumpur and beyond

At home, our applications to register as a non-governmental organisation were rejected again and again. We had no political ties, but an official pointed to my grandfather’s past as an opposition activist. So we registered in Malaysia and kept going.

Being pushed out made us global. Today our fellowships bring together young people from more than 100 countries, including Afghanistan, Palestine and Yemen. In 2021 Shomy and I were named to the Forbes 30 Under 30 Asia list.

IV

A birthday in Mukono

Bishop’s Senior School, Mukono, Uganda · 2022

I was invited to speak at a local school in Mukono. I didn’t know the students had planned a surprise: a birthday cake, songs, and some of the most precious memories of my life.

Ms. Grace Nabuduwa Musingo rallied young people there and supported our Awareness 360 team in the region. From Uganda to Kenya and Rwanda, I was welcomed with open arms and felt like a true global citizen.

“Have you ever felt truly blessed by the kindness of young people?”

Project Khale Hobe: cleaning a Dhaka canal by hand.

V

Coming home to clean up

Dhaka · 2023

I came back to Bangladesh to give something back. With volunteers, Asian Paints Bangladesh and our Awareness 360 team, we waded into one of the canals I grew up beside and cleared it out. We called the project Khale Hobe.

It hasn’t always been easy. In a system where corruption is normal, doing the right thing can drain you. But every cleared canal and every new volunteer is a reason to keep going.

Distributing relief in the hill tracts.

VI

Into the floods

Khagrachari and Rangamati · 2024

When the worst floods in three decades hit Bangladesh, I turned my birthday into a fundraiser. Then our team took trucks through nonstop rain, landslides and flash floods to indigenous communities in the hill tracts that relief efforts often overlook: Ujo Bazar, Beja Nandoram, Machalong Bazar.

With the Tripura Students Forum, we also ran a medical camp in Khagrachari for people with nowhere else to turn. It was one of the hardest journeys of my life.

Kigali, 2022 · Lancaster House, London, 2018 · Recognition from the Commonwealth Secretariat.

VII

A seat at the Commonwealth table

London · Kigali · 2018 – 2022

In 2018, at Lancaster House in London, I met Camilla, now Queen. I told her about our work across the Commonwealth, and left thinking about what “common wealth” should really mean and how it should be shared.

Four years later I was in Kigali on the Commonwealth Youth Forum’s International Taskforce, leading plenary programming for 500+ young delegates from 54 countries at the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting 2022. I spoke on the Forum’s inaugural panel, Taking Charge of Our Future, shared the stage with Rwanda’s Minister of Youth, Hon. Rosemary Mbabazi, and was honoured with a memento of recognition from the Commonwealth Secretariat.

Place de la République, Paris, 2022 · Dhaka, August 2024.

VIII

Raising my voice

Paris · Dhaka · 2022 – 2024

Some work happens in conference rooms. Some of it happens on the street. In July 2022, after the United States Supreme Court overturned Roe v. Wade, I joined activists at Place de la République in Paris to stand up for the right to safe, legal abortion, a matter of women’s health and human dignity.

Two summers later, my own country rose up. In July and August 2024, students led millions onto the streets of Bangladesh demanding justice. I stood with them at Shaheed Minar, and wrote about it in poems like Is the sky free?

IX

The rooms where decisions are made

Dubai · New Delhi · Lillehammer

I take what young people tell me into rooms where policy is made. At the 28th United Nations Climate Change Conference in Dubai I spoke with the United Nations Institute for Training and Research and Monash University on youth climate leadership. I met Shombi Sharp, the United Nations Resident Coordinator in India, and spoke at Gujarat’s Jal Utsav water festival. In Norway I joined the Young Experts Seminar on freedom of expression.

Earlier, I advised the United Nations Global Compact’s Target Gender Equality programme, working with 700+ companies in 45+ countries.

Keynote at Dhaka University.

X

Why not you?

Today

These days I spend a lot of time in classrooms and lecture halls: a keynote at Dhaka University, a guest lecture at BRAC University on why documenting failure matters, and a TEDx talk at Khulna University called “Why Not?”.

I’m also studying for a Master of Arts in Human Rights at the University of London on a Commonwealth scholarship. The message is the same everywhere I go.

“The world needs you to step up. Start where you are.”
